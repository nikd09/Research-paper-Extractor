# Wear Mechanisms & Failure Modes

## An experimental study of composite plain bearings: Influence of clearance on friction coefficient and temperature
- Adhesive wear
- Abrasive wear / surface smoothing
- Bearing seizure due to insufficient clearance (0.05 mm clearance)
- Uneven wear caused by axial misalignment between bearing and shaft
- Lowest dry running friction coefficient at 115 N load occurred at 0.15 mm clearance (µ = 0.184) followed by 0.9 mm clearance (µ = 0.192).
- PTFE lubrication significantly lowered friction coefficients compared to dry running.
- Clearance of 0.05 mm resulted in average friction coefficients of 0.782 to 0.819 (dry) and 0.636 to 0.650 (PTFE).

## A review: Enhancing tribological properties of journal bearings composite materials
- Adhesive wear
- Abrasive wear
- Transfer film peeling (under wet conditions)
- Scuffing
- Thermal residual stress cracking during interference assembly
- Bearing seizure during oil-cut situations
- Uneven radial clearance wear
- Plastic deformation under frictional heat
- Addition of PTFE and graphite to PEEK/CF significantly reduces friction and prevents scuffing.
- Increasing normal load generally decreases the friction coefficient of self-lubricating composites due to stable transfer film generation.
- Textured rubber surfaces lower average flow velocity and fluid film pressure fluctuations in water-lubricated journal bearings.

## MECHANICAL BEHAVIOR AND STRUCTURAL PERFORMANCE OF A Sn-CuSn-Pb COATED AND EPOXY COMPOSITE PLAIN BEARING
- Adhesive wear and scuffing during lubricant film breakdown or oil supply interruption
- Surface fatigue cracking in soft metallic overlays under cyclic loading
- Premature wear caused by insufficient oil film pressure or poor oil rheology
- Fatigue failure of soft white metal overlays under cyclic dynamic shaft loading
- Hydrodynamic oil film breakage at localized angular coordinates (~200°)
- Hydrodynamic pressure field peaks at an angular position of 150° and drops below supply pressure at 200° due to oil film rupture.

## Review and Prospect of Research Status on Sliding Bearing Coatings
- Abrasive wear
- Adhesive wear
- Micro-fracture
- Surface fatigue wear
- Cavitation wear
- Corrosion-wear synergy
- Seizure
- Spallation and flaking
- Interfacial delamination
- Surface fatigue cracking
- Pitting corrosion
- Thermal softening and melting
- Hydrodynamic lubrication in plain bearings yields ultra-low friction coefficients between 0.001 and 0.005.
- Boundary lubricated plain bearings exhibit friction coefficients ranging from 0.01 to 0.1.
- Pure PTFE exhibits friction coefficients of 0.02 to 0.04, but requires reinforcement due to low structural strength.
- Textured copper coatings reduce boundary lubrication friction coefficients from 0.12 down to 0.07-0.09.
- Phenolic/carbon fiber composites show an average friction coefficient 11.5% lower than epoxy/carbon fiber composites.
- A-C:H:W DLC combined with ionic liquid lubricant BMIM-PF6 achieves a 20% reduction in friction coefficient relative to bearing steel.

## The effect of irradiated PTFE on the friction and wear behavior of chemically bonded PA46-PTFE-cb and PA66-PTFE-cb compounds
- Abrasive wear of polymer compound block
- Transfer film build-up in surface grinding grooves
- Viscoelastic deformation and adhesion mediated by thermal accumulation at contact surface
- High volumetric wear rate under elevated contact pressure (5 MPa)
- Degradation of tribological performance due to uncoupled coarse PTFE agglomerates (non-irradiated MP1600)
- Friction coefficient drops rapidly during running-in (<2 km) and stabilizes into a quasi-stationary state after ~15 km sliding distance.
- Friction coefficient decreases with increasing contact pressure for PA66 and PA66-PTFE-cb compounds.
- PA-PTFE-cb compounds maintain lower steady-state COF than plain PA46 and PA66 across all test pressures.

## Tribological performances of polymeric plain bearings made of epoxy matrix with UHMWPE, MoS2, Kevlar and base oil as the fillers
- Adhesive wear
- Thermal softening and melting
- Scuffing and chunk removal
- Abrasive micro-scratching/grooving
- Transfer film formation on steel counterface
- Scuffing and catastrophic adhesive wear due to thermal softening at high PV limits (observed in MS composite beyond 37 MPa-m/s)
- COF generally decreases with increasing load and sliding velocity until thermal softening or failure occurs.
- Composite B exhibited the lowest COF across moderate to high PV factors, reaching 0.056 at 37 MPa-m/s.
- Composite K maintained a steady COF between 0.07 and 0.09 for PV values between 19.8 and 46.25 MPa-m/s, increasing to 0.13 at 52.5 MPa-m/s.
