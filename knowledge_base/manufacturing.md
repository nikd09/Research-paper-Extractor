# Manufacturing & Design Recommendations

## An experimental study of composite plain bearings: Influence of clearance on friction coefficient and temperature
- Accurate internal bore machining is critical; clearance tolerances below 0.15 mm risk heat accumulation and premature seizure.
- Bore dimensions should account for operational thermal expansion of polymer composites to prevent clearance loss.
- Misalignment between shaft and bearing axes causes severe local uneven wear.
- For solid-lubricated (PTFE) polymer bearings, design for an intermediate optimal clearance (e.g., ~0.5 mm for 34 mm shaft diameter) to balance contact pressure and thermal/seizure risks.
- Avoid ultra-tight clearances (<0.15 mm on 34 mm shafts) under continuous rotational sliding to prevent high friction spikes and rapid seizure.

## A review: Enhancing tribological properties of journal bearings composite materials
- Hand layup with fiber carding can be simplified for epoxy composites but may result in variable structural homogeneity.
- Powder metallurgy allows precise control of graphite content in copper-matrix journal bearings, where 1.0 wt% Gr yields optimal compressive strength and density.
- Stir-casting processes effectively incorporate ceramic particles (SiC, Fly ash) into copper and aluminum matrix bearings.
- Resin Transfer Molding (RTM) and braided structures enable self-lubricating hierarchical hybrid composite liners.
- Utilize carbon/phenol composites instead of carbon/epoxy in emergency oil-cut journal bearing designs to reduce friction during lubricant starvation.
- In corporate water-lubricated marine shafts, select rubber liners for light-load/low-speed regimes and Thordon SXL or Nordon 605 thermosets for high-load regimes.
- Incorporate 20% Glass Fiber + 25% PTFE into PA66 matrices to optimize wear behavior under elevated contact pressures.
- Keep tin content below 6% in aluminum journal bearing alloys to avoid excessive soft phase segregation without tribological gain.

## MECHANICAL BEHAVIOR AND STRUCTURAL PERFORMANCE OF A Sn-CuSn-Pb COATED AND EPOXY COMPOSITE PLAIN BEARING
- Coated plain bearings require a uniform 2 mm coating overlay fully connected/bonded onto a 38 mm substrate shell.
- Composite plain bearings can be fabricated as multi-layer laminated structures with specific fiber orientation layups (e.g. [0°, 45°]).
- Precision integration of oil feed features (70 mm feed slot, 16 mm groove width, three 14 mm diameter feed ports) is required to establish proper hydrodynamic lubricant pressure fields.
- Select Bronze (CuSn) overlay coatings over Tin (Sn) or Lead (Pb) when lower displacement (64% lower at 190°) and lower elastic deformation are critical under dynamic high-speed operation.
- Select Boron/Epoxy composites when designing composite journal bearings to achieve lower shear stress concentrations and minimized elastic deformation.
- Engineers should evaluate maximum mechanical stress concentrations between angular positions 120° and 250° (lower bearing generatrix) during design verification.
- Use composite bearings in applications where weight reduction and shear stress mitigation (up to 41% lower shear stress than coated metal bearings) outweigh tight deformation constraints.

## Review and Prospect of Research Status on Sliding Bearing Coatings
- Pulse electroplating (frequency 500-1000 Hz) enables uniform dispersion of hard nanoparticles in lead-free metal matrices, upgrading adhesion strength to 50-80 MPa while eliminating toxic lead.
- Laser cladding delivers metallurgical bonding strengths (150-200 MPa) ideal for remanufacturing and repairing worn heavy-duty main bearings, saving up to 60% of replacement costs.
- Magnetron sputtering offers precise thickness control and high bond strength (80-120 MPa) without producing wastewater, but exhibits low deposition throughput (0.5-2 m2/h) requiring multi-target or arc-assisted deposition for scaling.

- Deploy nano-CeO2 additions in carbide metal-ceramic coatings to reduce thermal mismatch stress and retard high-temperature oxidation.

## The effect of irradiated PTFE on the friction and wear behavior of chemically bonded PA46-PTFE-cb and PA66-PTFE-cb compounds
- Reactive melt extrusion requires precise temperature control (up to 330 °C in twin-screw extruders) to achieve full transamidation between PA matrices and irradiated PTFE micro powders.
- Electron beam irradiation (500 kGy) of PTFE micro powder is essential to generate peroxy radicals and functional end groups (-COF, -COOH) necessary for chemical bonding with polyamides.
- Injection molding cylinder temperatures must be tailored to matrix thermal properties (290 °C for PA66, 310 °C for PA46) to prevent thermal degradation during sample production.
- Select PA66-MP1100-cb or PA46-MP1200-cb combinations for optimal micro-powder dispersion and tribological performance under dry sliding.
- Design dry lubrication systems for operation at lower contact pressures (2-3 MPa) to maximize dry lubricant transfer film build-up efficiency relative to sacrificial component mass loss.
- Do not attempt to increase lubricant film transfer thickness merely by increasing normal load, as counterface surface topography roughness grooves saturate at modest wear volumes.

## Tribological performances of polymeric plain bearings made of epoxy matrix with UHMWPE, MoS2, Kevlar and base oil as the fillers
- When adding short Kevlar fibers to the liquid monomer resin, the mixture exhibits severe flowability issues due to extremely high viscosity; fibers must be pre-placed in the Teflon mould before pouring the resin mixture.
- Proper curing requires a multi-stage thermal process: 20 hours room temperature initial curing in a Teflon mould, followed by 5 hours high-temperature post-curing at 140 °C in an oven, and 12-15 hours desiccator stabilization.
- Circular bearing rings require fine abrasive paper polishing to ensure uniform contact line geometry prior to service.
- For moderate PV conditions (up to 37 MPa-m/s), in-situ base oil filled composite (Composite B) provides superior performance with minimal friction (0.056) and lowest wear rate.
- For severe PV conditions (37 to 52.5 MPa-m/s), Kevlar short fiber reinforced composite (Composite K) should be chosen due to its superior mechanical strength and scuffing resistance.
- Ensure structural component designs account for reduced glass transition temperature (Tg = 94-99 °C) when liquid lubricants or aramid fibers are incorporated into epoxy matrices.
